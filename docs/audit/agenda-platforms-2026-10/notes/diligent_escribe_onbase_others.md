# How Diligent (Community/iCompass, BoardDocs), eSCRIBE, Hyland OnBase Agenda, and other non-Granicus/CivicPlus agenda platforms handle uploaded tagged PDFs

Research date: 2026-10-08. Public sources only, no sign-ins. Labels used below: **[Vendor claim]**, **[Independent observation]** (third party or municipal), and **[Inference]** (my own reasoning).

Method note: I tried to download sample attachments from BoardDocs (`boarddocs.com/.../$file/...pdf`), eSCRIBE (`pub-*.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx`), and eSCRIBE's own e-publishing site, to inspect `/StructTreeRoot` and Producer metadata. All three returned HTTP 403 to the fetch tool, so **I made no byte-level checks of what any platform actually serves**. Every "served as-is" statement below rests on vendor documentation or URL patterns, not file inspection.

## 1. Are individual attachments published as separate links (served as-is), in addition to or instead of a merged packet?

### Takeaway
On Diligent Community/iCompass and BoardDocs, each uploaded attachment is offered to the public as its own link. Diligent states in writing that linked attachments are "as accessible as they were when uploaded," which strongly implies the original file is passed through unchanged. BoardDocs has historically not merged attachments into its printed packet at all. OnBase Agenda Online and eSCRIBE publish both a merged packet and the agenda with links to supporting documents. At least one OnBase deployment (Anchorage) has had periods when the individual links did not work and the packet was the only route. No source I found confirms byte-for-byte serving on any platform.

### Cited Findings
- **Diligent Community (2023 fact sheet) [Vendor claim]:** "Any documents uploaded by customers and linked as attachments will be as accessible as they were when uploaded. Content entered into the system is the responsibility of the customer to ensure accessibility." The same sheet also says Diligent is not responsible for documents "migrated from prior systems." — [Diligent Community ADA Accessibility fact sheet, ©2023 (PDF; text extracted locally)](https://connect.diligent.com/file-asset/Diligent_Community_ADA_Accessibility_Fa?v=1)
- **Diligent Community DocAccess help page [Vendor claim]:** "The original PDF document remains unchanged and part of the official record". Users can open the DocAccess HTML view instead of the standard PDF viewer, and the original PDF stays available. — [Diligent One help: DocAccess for Diligent Community](https://help.diligentoneplatform.com/helpdocs/d1p/en-us/Content/community/getting-started/community-docaccess.htm)
- **BoardDocs [Vendor FAQ, 2017]:** "BoardDocs does not restrict the type of attachments that can be placed in the system. However, we recommend attaching files in PDF … format." Printing the "entire packet" works "although without attachments. Attachments would need to printed individually." — [NJSBA-hosted BoardDocs FAQ (PDF, created June 2017; text extracted locally)](https://www.njsba.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/BoardDocsFAQ.pdf)
- **BoardDocs public interface [Independent: district guide]:** the agenda detail view shows "document links" for attachments, which open for viewing and printing. The meeting cover page has "Download Agenda as PDF" and "Print the Agenda" buttons. — [District BoardDocs User Guide (S3-hosted)](https://s3.amazonaws.com/scschoolfiles/4370/boarddocs_guide.pdf); [Thrillshare-hosted "How to navigate BoardDocs"](https://files-backend.assets.thrillshare.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/3721/Mcps/99bff797-a928-4895-92dd-96e7a69ef80e/Directions_for_Navigating_BoardDocs_in_Public_.pdf?disposition=inline) (both from search snippets; not fully read)
- **BoardDocs attachment URL pattern [Independent observation]:** public BoardDocs attachments are served from per-file URLs of the form `boarddocs.com/<state>/<org>/Board.nsf/files/<ID>/$file/<original filename>.pdf`. Example: [PAUSD resolution agreement](https://www.boarddocs.com/ca/pausd/Board.nsf/files/AJKTKK77C09B/$file/20170214PAUSDOCRResolutionAgreement.pdf). The original filename is kept in the URL.
- **OnBase Agenda (Hyland docs, Foundation 22.1) [Vendor doc]:** "Agenda packets contain the agenda and all supporting documents for each agenda item in one PDF file." Packet configuration includes "creating and configuring text overlays." Agenda Online lets the public view agenda, minutes, summary, and packet documents. — [Hyland docs: Configuring Packets (22.1)](https://docs.hyland.com/r/OnBase/Agenda/English/Foundation-22.1/Agenda/Configuration/Configuring-Packets); [Hyland: Using Agenda Online (22.1)](https://support.hyland.com/r/OnBase/Agenda/English/Foundation-22.1/Agenda/Usage/Using-Agenda-Online)
- **OnBase Agenda 25.1 [Vendor doc, via search snippet]:** an uploaded supporting document "is uploaded into OnBase and added to your agenda item as a supporting document." An "Agenda Item Packet" is a separate generated collection of one item's supporting documents. — [Hyland docs: Agenda 25.1](https://docs.hyland.com/r/OnBase/Agenda/Foundation-25.1/Agenda/Usage/Logging-On) (content quoted in a search summary; the page I fetched only showed the logon topic)
- **OnBase in practice [Independent: municipal notice]:** an Anchorage notice about its move to OnBase warned that links to individual attachments inside the agenda may be inactive, so readers had to open the full Agenda Packet and search or scroll it. — [Municipality of Anchorage, "OnBase is the new Agenda and Meeting Management…" (PDF)](https://www.muni.org/Residents/Documents/Agenda%20Evolution%20Communication_For%20More%20Information%20Final.pdf) (from a search snippet; not fetched in full)
- **OnBase / Tampa [Independent: journalist, Nov 12 2023]:** supporting documents appear as links with machine-style filenames, for example "…623 E7TH AVE SUPPORTING DOCUMENT.PDF". — [The Tampa Monitor, "First Thoughts On New Agenda Platform Hyland Onbase"](https://tampamonitor.com/tampa-city-council/first-thoughts-on-new-agenda-platform-hyland-onbase/)
- **eSCRIBE [Vendor marketing]:** "Past and upcoming meetings, agendas, minutes, and supporting documents live in one searchable repository". The agenda goes to the public portal "in the same action that publishes it to the council." — [eSCRIBE Online Publishing & Meeting Portal](https://www.escribemeetings.com/citizen-engagement/online-publishing/)
- **eSCRIBE [Independent: city memo, Royal Oak MI, Nov 1 2023]:** "When fully integrated eScribe offers us the option to publish both a PDF version of the packet and an HTML version." The subscription agreement says eScribe stores customer content "in native file format (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, MP4)" and will return it on request. — [Royal Oak, MI: Approval of Subscription Agreement eScribe (PDF; text extracted locally)](https://www.romi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/37188/eScribe-Subscription-Agreement-November-13-2023)
- **AgendaQuick (Destiny Software) [Vendor marketing, undated]:** "Produce your entire packet, completely bookmarked with a single click." Users can "Quickly add supporting documents, maps, photos, digital audio & digital video" and "publish your agenda, your complete packet." Published agendas and minutes come in both HTML and PDF. — [AgendaQuick product page](https://www.destinysoftwareinc.com/agendaquick/)

### Inferences
- [Inference] On Diligent Community, iCompass, and BoardDocs, a clerk who uploads an Ada Editor PDF/UA file as an item attachment most likely gets that same file served at its own URL. Diligent's statement that attachments stay "as accessible as they were when uploaded," BoardDocs' filename-preserving `$file` URLs, and DocAccess leaving originals "unchanged" all point that way. Still unverified: whether any optimizer or virus scanner rewrites bytes on upload.
- [Inference] On OnBase, eSCRIBE, and AgendaQuick, the individual supporting-document link is probably the original upload, and the "packet" is a separately generated merge. Residents who follow the packet link see the merged file, not the clerk's tagged original.

### Gaps
- I found no vendor or independent byte-level test (for example, a hash comparison or a PAC/veraPDF run) of attachments downloaded from any of these platforms. Downloads were blocked (403) for me.
- I couldn't confirm whether eSCRIBE individual attachment links (`filestream.ashx?DocumentId=`) serve the original upload or an eSCRIBE-converted PDF.
- I couldn't confirm whether current BoardDocs (2025–2026) has added a merged-packet download for the public. The "no attachments in packet" statement dates from 2017.

## 2. How is the packet compiled, and does the vendor say tags are preserved, dropped, or re-generated? Any accessible-packet, auto-tagging, or remediation feature?

### Takeaway
Diligent is the only vendor that addresses tags in its packet output. It says its PDF processing "do[es] their best at maintaining the accessibility of the source documents" but "cannot be relied upon to create fully accessible PDF documents." It adds bookmarks and OCRs scanned pages. Diligent's 2026 remediation answer is DocAccess, a separately licensed CivicPlus add-on that builds an HTML alternative beside the unchanged PDF; it does not repair tags. Hyland documents one-PDF packets with "text overlays" and is silent on tags. eSCRIBE's accessibility marketing is advice to staff plus a portal WCAG claim; I found no auto-tagging or packet remediation feature, and its own WCAG page contained placeholder text. AgendaQuick promises bookmarked packets and says nothing about tags.

### Cited Findings
- **Diligent Community compiled PDFs [Vendor claim, 2023]:** "To create these documents, we process HTML, pdf, Word and other formatted documents (many of which have been uploaded by our customers) into printable PDF documents. Our processing tools do their best at maintaining the accessibility of the source documents as the PDF output documents are created, but cannot be relied upon to create fully accessible PDF documents. Our PDF agenda documents have bookmarks added to assist in navigation and we will auto OCR flat scanned PDFs to improve accessibility." — [Diligent Community ADA Accessibility fact sheet (2023)](https://connect.diligent.com/file-asset/Diligent_Community_ADA_Accessibility_Fa?v=1)
- **Diligent fact sheet as a file [Independent observation, low confidence]:** the fetch tool reported that the fact-sheet PDF declares `/Lang(en-GB)` but showed no `/StructTreeRoot` or `/MarkInfo` in the catalog it could see. The file was truncated, so this is inconclusive. — [same URL](https://connect.diligent.com/file-asset/Diligent_Community_ADA_Accessibility_Fa?v=1)
- **Diligent DocAccess [Vendor claim]:** "DocAccess automatically creates an accessible HTML alternative for supported public PDF documents." It applies to attachments in agendas, minutes, and policies, the document library, global search, and split-screen views. It "is available as a separately licensed product and is not included with Diligent Community by default." It "does not guarantee compliance for the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)." The page doesn't mention tags or PDF/UA. Word, Excel, and PowerPoint files "continue to behave as they do today." — [Diligent help: DocAccess for Diligent Community](https://help.diligentoneplatform.com/helpdocs/d1p/en-us/Content/community/getting-started/community-docaccess.htm)
- **Diligent + CivicPlus DocAccess, Sept 22 2026 [Vendor press release]:** "Available with Diligent Community, BoardDocs and iCompass." It "detects PDFs uploaded to agenda packets, supporting documents and libraries" and "generates an accessible HTML experience alongside the original." It covers archived and new PDFs. The release gives no pricing and names no Title II deadline. — [Business Wire via FinancialContent, 2026-09-22](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/bizwire-2026-9-22-diligent-announces-integration-with-civicplus-docaccess-helping-public-sector-organizations-expand-access-to-supporting-documents-at-scale)
- **OnBase Agenda packets [Vendor doc]:** packets are "one PDF file" containing the agenda and all supporting documents. Packet setup includes "creating and configuring text overlays." The overview page doesn't mention tags or accessibility. — [Hyland docs: Configuring Packets (Foundation 22.1)](https://docs.hyland.com/r/OnBase/Agenda/English/Foundation-22.1/Agenda/Configuration/Configuring-Packets)
- **OnBase solution sheet [Vendor marketing, via search snippet]:** the system builds the packet automatically and adds ordinance/resolution numbers, page numbers, and placeholder pages. It also produces public and executive-session packets. — [Hyland OnBase Government Agenda Management solution summary (PDF; returned 404 when fetched)](https://www.hyland.com/-/media/Project/Hyland/Hyland/solutionsheet/OnBase-Government-SolutionSummary-Agenda-Management-4102-70-20-019006.pdf); an integrator page says the same — [ISSI OnBase Agenda Management](https://www.issi-online.com/document-capture-management-workflow-automation-rpa-solutions-software/hyland/onbase/agenda-management)
- **Hyland WCAG posture [Vendor listing, via search snippet]:** Hyland "strives for Level AA conformance" and says it cannot give every interface WCAG conformance "due to the nature of certain modules." — [UK G-Cloud Hyland listing](https://www.applytosupply.digitalmarketplace.service.gov.uk/g-cloud/services/747474755144311) (snippet; not fully read; may not cover Agenda specifically)
- **eSCRIBE WCAG page [Vendor marketing, undated, footer "Copyright @ 2026 OnBoard"]:**
  - The only document-related "what we are doing" item is "PDF accessibility guidance: Clear instructions for staff uploading accessible documents."
  - It says "eScribe's Public Site is on track for WCAG 2.1 AA certification by April 2026" and promises "VPAT documentation" and "third-party audits."
  - The "Word Integration" feature description was literally "Lorem ipsum dolor sit amet."
  - The page names no auto-tagging, remediation, or accessible-packet feature.
  - Source: [eSCRIBE "WCAG 2.1 AA Compliance for Government Meeting Portals"](https://www.escribemeetings.com/web-content-accessibility-guidelines-2-1/)
- **eSCRIBE claim conflict [Vendor marketing]:** the Online Publishing page says "The portal is WCAG 2.1 AA certified," the WCAG page says "on track … by April 2026," and an older Platform Overview says "WCAG 2.0 compliant." — [Online Publishing](https://www.escribemeetings.com/citizen-engagement/online-publishing/); [WCAG page](https://www.escribemeetings.com/web-content-accessibility-guidelines-2-1/); [Platform Overview](https://www.escribemeetings.com/platform-overview/)
- **eSCRIBE checklist blog [Vendor content]:** says every published PDF "must be tagged with headings, lists, and table structures." This is advice to customers, not a product behavior. — [eSCRIBE WCAG Compliance Checklist for the Public Meeting Lifecycle](https://www.escribemeetings.com/blog/wcag-compliance-checklist-public-meeting-lifecycle/) (from a search snippet)
- **eSCRIBE ownership [Press]:** OnBoard (Passageways) acquired eSCRIBE in August 2021. — [PR Newswire, 2021](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/onboard-acquires-leading-public-sector-meeting-management-solution-escribe-301347807.html); [OnBoard page](https://www.onboardmeetings.com/escribe/)
- **AgendaQuick [Vendor marketing]:** "entire packet, completely bookmarked with a single click." The page has no accessibility, ADA, or WCAG statement. — [AgendaQuick](https://www.destinysoftwareinc.com/agendaquick/)
- **Laserfiche (SIRE successor / Agenda Manager) [Independent: Laserfiche reseller CPS, Dec 2 2025]:** "Laserfiche ensures that its viewing environment behaves accessibly, but it does not remediate uploaded content." "A PDF without tags stays untagged." Agencies "remain responsible for ensuring that documents displayed in Laserfiche meet WCAG standards." — [CPS, "WCAG Compliance and Laserfiche"](https://www.cps247.com/post/wcag-compliance-and-laserfiche-what-agencies-need-to-know-about-accessibility)
- **Laserfiche Agenda Manager [Independent: user forum, 2014, via search snippet]:** a user asked whether the Agenda Manager PDF generated from Word "meets the Accessibility Standards," noting that it is produced with PDF Factory (a print-driver PDF tool). I didn't see the answer. — [Laserfiche Answers, Agenda-Manager topic](https://answers.laserfiche.com/questions/topic/1/Agenda-Manager). Laserfiche publishes ACRs for its products at [accessibility.laserfiche.com](https://accessibility.laserfiche.com/).
- **General mechanism [Independent: Adobe community expert]:** flattening or optimizing can rasterize text, and "it is simply impossible to preserve tagging over such changes." — [Adobe Community thread on losing tags with PDF Optimizer](https://community.adobe.com/t5/acrobat-discussions/loosing-the-tags-from-accessible-pdf-while-using-pdf-optimizer/m-p/10943870) (from a search snippet)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Any compiled packet from Diligent, OnBase, eSCRIBE, or AgendaQuick should be assumed to lose or degrade the tags of an Ada Editor PDF/UA attachment:
  - Diligent's own wording admits its tool "cannot be relied upon."
  - OnBase adds text overlays and page numbers. Unless these are written as Artifacts and the structure trees are merged, the result is untagged or partly tagged.
  - Partly tagged merges are a known failure mode. Combining tagged and untagged PDFs yields a partially tagged, inaccessible file.
  - None of these vendors claims a PDF/UA-conformant packet.
- [Inference] Diligent's 2026 strategy is to sidestep packet tagging with an HTML alternative (DocAccess) rather than fix PDFs. That makes the Ada Editor original, served as a separate attachment, the best accessible PDF a resident can reach on Diligent platforms. Even DocAccess HTML is machine-generated and may be worse than a well-tagged source.
- [Inference] eSCRIBE's "accessibility for AODA" positioning, as far as public material shows, covers the HTML portal plus guidance telling staff to upload accessible files. I found no evidence of auto-tagging or remediation of PDFs.

### Gaps
- No vendor (Diligent, Hyland, eSCRIBE, Destiny, Laserfiche) publicly documents which PDF library it uses to merge packets, whether it merges structure trees, or whether it writes page-number stamps as Artifacts.
- I couldn't retrieve Hyland's "Creating a Meeting Packet Template" or "Configuring Text Overlays" pages, or the Agenda 25.1 "Accessibility Features" and "Packet Export" pages.
- I found no Diligent, BoardDocs, OnBase, or eSCRIBE ACR/VPAT publicly posted. Diligent's 2023 fact sheet references a "Diligent Community Accessibility Conformance Report," but I didn't locate it.
- I couldn't confirm whether eSCRIBE met its April 2026 WCAG 2.1 AA certification target.

## 3. Are Word uploads converted to PDF server-side, and is that conversion tagged?

### Takeaway
Diligent Community converts Word (and HTML) into PDF for its compiled outputs and disclaims full accessibility of that output. DocAccess explicitly does not handle Word. BoardDocs accepts any file type and recommends that clerks upload PDF. eSCRIBE stores Word natively and builds staff reports from Word templates, but I found no public description of its PDF conversion or whether it tags. For Ada Editor, the safe course is for the clerk to upload the tagged PDF, not a Word file.

### Cited Findings
- **Diligent [Vendor claim, 2023]:** "we process HTML, pdf, Word and other formatted documents … into printable PDF documents … [tools] cannot be relied upon to create fully accessible PDF documents." — [Diligent fact sheet](https://connect.diligent.com/file-asset/Diligent_Community_ADA_Accessibility_Fa?v=1)
- **Diligent DocAccess [Vendor doc]:** for Word, Excel, and PowerPoint, "These file types continue to behave as they do today within Diligent Community". These files get no HTML alternative. The same page lists them under "supported public PDF files, such as," which looks like a documentation inconsistency. — [DocAccess help page](https://help.diligentoneplatform.com/helpdocs/d1p/en-us/Content/community/getting-started/community-docaccess.htm)
- **BoardDocs [Vendor FAQ, 2017]:** no file-type restriction; PDF is recommended "to ensure portability across many types of devices." — [BoardDocs FAQ](https://www.njsba.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/BoardDocsFAQ.pdf)
- **eSCRIBE [Independent: contract]:** customer content is stored "in native file format (Word, Excel, PowerPoint, PDF, MP4)." — [Royal Oak eScribe agreement, 2023](https://www.romi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/37188/eScribe-Subscription-Agreement-November-13-2023)
- **OnBase/Tampa [Independent, 2023]:** the "Standard View" of the agenda appears to be HTML generated from a Word document without the generated markup being stripped. — [Tampa Monitor](https://tampamonitor.com/tampa-city-council/first-thoughts-on-new-agenda-platform-hyland-onbase/)
- **Laserfiche Agenda Manager [Independent, 2014 forum snippet]:** agenda PDFs are generated from Word via PDF Factory. — [Laserfiche Answers](https://answers.laserfiche.com/questions/topic/1/Agenda-Manager)
- **OnBoard (eSCRIBE parent's own board product) [Vendor help]:** agenda sections accept Word, PowerPoint, Excel, and PDF, and images must be converted to PDF first. This is a different product from eSCRIBE. — [Passageways/OnBoard help](https://help.passageways.com/hc/en-us/articles/360038266971-Creating-the-Meeting-s-Agenda-and-Uploading-Documents) (snippet)

### Inferences
- [Inference] Where a platform renders Word to PDF for packets (Diligent explicitly; likely OnBase and eSCRIBE), the converter is server-side and generic. Print-driver tools such as PDF Factory generally produce untagged PDFs. Ada Editor users should export tagged PDF and upload the PDF, never the .docx.
- [Inference] If a platform converts uploaded Word attachments into PDF for the public link itself (unconfirmed for any product here), the resident would get an untagged derivative. That is a further reason to upload PDF.

### Gaps
- I found no documentation on whether eSCRIBE, OnBase, or BoardDocs convert an uploaded .docx attachment to PDF for the public link, or serve the .docx itself.
- I don't know which conversion engines (Aspose, Muhimbi, LibreOffice, Office Online Server) any vendor uses, or their tagging settings.

## 4. Does the platform offer an HTML agenda/minutes view as the accessible alternative?

### Takeaway
Yes, all of them do to some degree, and each vendor positions the HTML view as its accessibility answer: Diligent Community (tested to at least WCAG 2.0 AA per the vendor), eSCRIBE (HTML plus PDF), AgendaQuick (HTML plus PDF), BoardDocs (web agenda), and OnBase Agenda Online ("Accessible View"). An independent 2023 review of Tampa's OnBase deployment found the HTML agenda rendered client-side and poorly structured. The "Accessible View" toggle mostly added CSS classes.

### Cited Findings
- **Diligent Community [Vendor claim]:** "all Diligent Community web pages are created and displayed with HTML technology". "HTML published Agendas" and "HTML published Minutes" are "tested and compliant to at least WCAG 2.0 AA level." Diligent also states "Diligent Community meets WCAG 2.1 AA compliance requirements." — [Diligent fact sheet, 2023](https://connect.diligent.com/file-asset/Diligent_Community_ADA_Accessibility_Fa?v=1)
- **OnBase Agenda Online [Vendor doc]:** "Accessible View is used in the Agenda Online portal. Provides the ability for the use of a document reader to aid in reading documents." — [Hyland Agenda Definitions (22.1)](https://support.hyland.com/r/OnBase/Agenda/English/Foundation-22.1/Agenda/Usage/Definitions) (definition quoted in search snippet)
- **OnBase / Tampa [Independent: Michael Bishop, Tampa Monitor, Nov 12 2023]:**
  - "The single biggest problem is that the agenda itself is rendered client side." Viewing source shows none of the agenda content.
  - The "Accessible View" appears to add classes such as "accessible-header" to the same markup.
  - The page has multiple `<h1>` elements.
  - The page source showed "Version: 22.1.11.1000."
  - Source: [Tampa Monitor](https://tampamonitor.com/tampa-city-council/first-thoughts-on-new-agenda-platform-hyland-onbase/)
- **eSCRIBE [Vendor sales deck / city memo]:** HTML and PDF publishing. — [Royal Oak memo, 2023](https://www.romi.gov/DocumentCenter/View/37188/eScribe-Subscription-Agreement-November-13-2023); [eSCRIBE presentation to Kitchener (snippet)](https://epublishing.escribemeetings.com/filestream.ashx?DocumentId=4540)
- **AgendaQuick [Vendor claim]:** "Published agendas and minutes are available in both html and PDF so citizens can choose." — [Destiny Software AgendaQuick](https://www.destinysoftwareinc.com/agendaquick/) (wording per search snippet; the fetched page confirmed one-click posting of agendas and packets)
- **BoardDocs:** the public agenda is a web view with an optional "Download Agenda as PDF." — [Thrillshare-hosted BoardDocs guide](https://files-backend.assets.thrillshare.com/documents/asset/uploaded_file/3721/Mcps/99bff797-a928-4895-92dd-96e7a69ef80e/Directions_for_Navigating_BoardDocs_in_Public_.pdf?disposition=inline) (snippet)

### Inferences
- [Inference] HTML agendas cover the agenda text only. Supporting documents (staff reports, ordinances, contracts), which are what Ada Editor produces, remain PDF attachments on every platform. Their accessibility depends on the uploaded file being served intact, or on an add-on like DocAccess.

### Gaps
- I found no independent WCAG audit of Diligent Community, BoardDocs, eSCRIBE, or AgendaQuick public portals. The Tampa piece is a single 2023 journalist review of one OnBase deployment.

## 5. Vendor accessibility statements, VPATs/ACRs, release notes, clerk posts, complaints or lawsuits

### Takeaway
Every vendor that addresses the issue puts responsibility for attachment accessibility on the customer: Diligent explicitly, and Laserfiche through its reseller. None publishes a public PDF/UA claim for packets. I found no lawsuit or OCR resolution agreement that specifically cites BoardDocs, iCompass, eSCRIBE, or OnBase packets. BoardDocs appears in OCR materials mainly as the host of district documents.

### Inferences
- [Inference] Vendors' contractual and marketing language makes the clerk responsible for attachments. A small Florida town's practical protection is to (a) link the original tagged attachment, not just the packet, and (b) test the served file with PAC or veraPDF after posting.
- [Inference] DocAccess's arrival on Diligent platforms in September 2026 shows that agencies and vendors see uploaded PDFs as the remaining Title II gap. It competes with Ada Editor's value proposition only partly: it produces HTML, not tagged PDFs, and is a paid add-on.
