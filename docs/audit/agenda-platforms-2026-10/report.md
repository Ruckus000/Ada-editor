# Agenda packets break what attachments preserve

On most of the platforms Florida towns use, a tagged PDF/UA file that a clerk uploads **as a PDF** reaches the public with its tags intact through the item's own attachment link. Inside the platform-compiled agenda packet, the same file almost never stays accessible. If the clerk uploads the Word file instead, the tags are lost before publication. The evidence comes from four research threads. One inspected **23 public files from 9 Florida municipalities on 7 platforms**. The other three spot-checked tenants out of state (Boulder CO, Burbank CA, Dubuque IA, Wethersfield CT, Battle Creek MI) and read vendor documentation. On the attachment link, tag trees survived on Legistar, CivicClerk, OnBase, Peak and CivicPlus Agenda Center. On Legistar the file is rewritten, but its tags, alt text and bookmarks were kept. PrimeGov/OneMeeting split: Oviedo FL passed a tagged upload through, while every uploaded PDF sampled at Boulder CO came back stripped. Every compiled packet that was inspected damaged the tags. Municode Meetings (2 of 2 Florida towns) and PrimeGov/OneMeeting (Oviedo and Boulder) discarded the attachments' tag trees. Diligent Community (Oldsmar, one meeting) re-tagged the whole package as flat paragraphs and still declared PDF/UA-1. CivicClerk (four packets) kept source trees, headings included, but dropped the document language and title and stamped page numbers that are neither tagged nor marked as artifacts. Ada Editor's promise therefore holds at the file level but not at the packet level. No vendor claims packet conformance, and Granicus, Diligent and Laserfiche explicitly leave attachment accessibility to the customer. The near-term moves are clear clerk guidance, a post-publication checker and one cheap hardening of Ada's export. A full packet builder pays off only where the clerk controls the packet file, and it would force Ada to handle untagged third-party PDFs. Caveats: eSCRIBE, BoardDocs, Novus and Granicus Peak in Florida remain untested, and no veraPDF or PAC run was made on any platform output. Every finding below comes from structural inspection (StructTreeRoot, MarkInfo, ParentTree, MCIDs, producer strings).

## Individual attachment links pass tags through, with two exceptions

Where a platform exposes the clerk's uploaded PDF as its own link, the tags generally survived. **CivicClerk** served uploaded PDFs as-is in Alachua and Minneola. The public API's `pdfVersionFullPath` points to the same blob as the original upload, and the served file sizes match the listed sizes. A tagged staff report kept its 61 elements, its H1–H3 headings and its EN-US language ([Alachua meeting API](https://alachuafl.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/834); [Minneola meeting API](https://minneolafl.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/1149)). A second researcher saw the same same-blob pattern at Dubuque IA ([Dubuque meeting 216 JSON](https://dubuqueia.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/216)). **OnBase Agenda Online** in Tampa served an Acrobat-OCR'd, tagged memo unchanged, one file from one meeting ([Tampa O1](https://tampagov.hylandcloud.com/251agendaonline/Documents/DownloadFileBytes/CRA%20MEMO-TAMPAHEIGHTSROUNDABOUTANDSLIPLANEFIX(FILENO.CRA26-26393%3bONBASENO.26393).PDF.pdf?documentType=1&meetingId=2934&itemId=26393&publishId=214180&isSection=False&isAttachment=True)). **CivicPlus Agenda Center** passed uploads through with their original producers in both places checked. Lake Placid FL's file kept its Acrobat producer, and a Canva-made tagged agenda at Wethersfield CT kept its tags and `/Lang` ([Lake Placid](https://www.lakeplacidfl.net/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_09212026-73); [Wethersfield](https://www.wethersfieldct.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_09282026-669)). **Granicus Peak** has no Florida instance that either researcher could find. At Burbank CA it served tagged attachments with their original producers and no post-processing stamp ([Burbank meta_id=481490](https://burbank.granicus.com/MetaViewer.php?view_id=6&event_id=9895&meta_id=481490)).

**Legistar** needed reconciling. The Florida researcher pulled Delray Beach attachments from the stored-file path (`legistar1.com/.../attachments/`) and found tagged Word PDFs "served as uploaded" ([Delray L2](https://delraybeach.legistar1.com/delraybeach/attachments/0e22273d-b205-43fa-ab98-663a8b05febc.pdf)). The Granicus researcher compared that stored path with the public InSite link a resident actually clicks (`View.ashx`) for a Fort Lauderdale meeting. The two were **not byte-identical**. InSite re-saves every PDF through **PDFlib PLOP 4.1**, linearizes it, and overwrites both the Info Title and the XMP `dc:title` with "<File #> - <attachment name>". Even so, the tagged exhibit kept all **515 structure elements, its 5 `/Alt` entries and its bookmarks** ([served copy](https://fortlauderdale.legistar.com/View.ashx?M=F&ID=15972073&GUID=36D551A1-47D0-40FC-87C9-CF76B2E5B236&G=F11871F6-88F5-40B5-BD94-B6711694F9A7) vs. [stored original](https://legistar.granicus.com/FortLauderdale/attachments/cbe4c2c1-7309-49f8-a428-be3c7b2c4b09.pdf)). The hash comparison is the stronger evidence about what the public receives, but the two findings agree on the point that matters: tags survive. One Ada-specific risk remains untested. None of the sampled originals carried a `pdfuaid:part` identifier or `DisplayDocTitle`, so nobody knows whether PLOP preserves them. The title overwrite also replaces the document title Ada's author set.

**PrimeGov/OneMeeting is the first exception, and the evidence conflicts.** In Oviedo FL (one meeting), an Acrobat-tagged attachment came back with its Acrobat producer and a 750-element tag tree ([Oviedo G3](https://cityofoviedo.primegov.com/api/compilemeetingattachmenthistory/historyattachment/?historyId=45a88977-544b-41f3-aa25-a3a0b0a1797c)). In Boulder CO (meetings from January and September 2026), **every uploaded PDF sampled had been re-written by Aspose.PDF for .NET 25.10.0** and came back with no StructTreeRoot and no MarkInfo. Hundreds of orphaned MCIDs per file showed the originals had been tagged ([Boulder Ordinance 8731](https://bouldercolorado.primegov.com/api/compilemeetingattachmenthistory/historyattachment/?historyId=9df078d2-f3fc-494b-910e-9ea7e7973099); [Boulder Ordinance 8764, Sept 2026](https://bouldercolorado.primegov.com/api/compilemeetingattachmenthistory/historyattachment/?historyId=9465c28b-b8ab-415e-9939-34dfb68e2bf9)). Both results are direct file inspections of comparable strength. Each covers one tenant, the platform version was the same, and both date from September 2026. The most likely explanation is therefore a per-tenant setting, not a version difference. That is an inference. Granicus's login-gated 2026 release notes, seen only as a search snippet, say "we resolved an issue wherein the attachments could lose their accessibility features during publishing", yet Boulder files published 2026-09-24 were still stripped ([What's New in Agenda OE 2026](https://support.granicus.com/s/article/Whats-New-in-OneMeeting?language=en_US)). Treat OneMeeting attachment links as unreliable until each customer town has been checked.

**Municode Meetings is the second exception, because it has no attachment link at all.** All three sampled Florida towns (Eatonville, Loxahatchee Groves, Dundee) publish exactly two files per meeting, "Agenda" and "Agenda Packet", with no per-item attachment links ([Eatonville meeting page](https://eatonville-fl.municodemeetings.com/bc-towncouncil/page/regular-council-meeting-73); [Dundee meeting page](https://dundee-fl.municodemeetings.com/bc-tc/page/town-commission-meeting-75)). The separately posted Agenda was served as the clerk's tagged Word PDF ([Eatonville agenda](https://mccmeetings.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/eatnvllefl-pubu/MEET-Agenda-c729a91245174ffba670a29e1f8ad7d6.pdf)), so an Ada-made agenda would survive. An Ada-made staff report or ordinance reaches the public only through the packet, and the next section shows what happens to it there.

**Diligent Community/iCompass** sits between the two cases, and here the vendor's claim and the observation diverge. Diligent's 2023 fact sheet says documents "linked as attachments will be as accessible as they were when uploaded" ([Diligent fact sheet](https://connect.diligent.com/file-asset/Diligent_Community_ADA_Accessibility_Fa?v=1)). Oldsmar FL (one meeting), however, exposed no raw uploads. Each item link returned a PDF that Diligent had built with ABCpdf: a cover "Agenda Item Report" followed by the attachments. In the legal-invoices item, only the cover page was tagged ([Oldsmar I3](https://myoldsmar.civicweb.net/document/254632/Consider%20approving%20payment%20to%20legal%20counsel%20for.pdf?handle=E863AE9DAC0C4CFA999BFC64E17D0B06)). The observation is stronger evidence for what Oldsmar residents receive. The vendor claim may still hold for other tenant configurations. Diligent-owned BoardDocs serves attachments from per-file URLs that keep the original filename ([PAUSD example](https://www.boarddocs.com/ca/pausd/Board.nsf/files/AJKTKK77C09B/$file/20170214PAUSDOCRResolutionAgreement.pdf)). No researcher could download a BoardDocs or eSCRIBE file, because both sites blocked requests with HTTP 403, and pass-through on those platforms is unverified.

## Every compiled packet tested lost, flattened or broke the tags

All five platform-compiled packet families sampled in Florida came out of a commercial .NET PDF library. CivicClerk, PrimeGov and Municode Meetings use **Aspose.PDF**, and Diligent uses **ABCpdf**. The results differed by vendor, not by library: CivicClerk and Municode both use Aspose.PDF and produced opposite outcomes. Tag loss is therefore a configuration choice each vendor made, not a property of the PDF format.

**Municode Meetings drops the tag tree: 2 of 2 paired Florida meetings.** At Eatonville, the agenda was posted separately as a tagged Word 2016 PDF (58 elements, Table/TR/TD, en-US). In the packet the same agenda is page 1, and the packet catalog has **no StructTreeRoot and no MarkInfo**. Twenty-six of its 38 pages still carry `/StructParents`, and 691 MCID sequences point at nothing ([Eatonville packet](https://mccmeetings.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/eatnvllefl-pubu/MEET-Packet-c729a91245174ffba670a29e1f8ad7d6.pdf)). Loxahatchee Groves showed the same pattern on all six pages. The packet adds "Item 1." headers and page numbers ([Loxahatchee packet](https://mccmeetings.blob.core.usgovcloudapi.net/loxahatch-pubu/MEET-Packet-6399a99597fc4849a0367f0151736d10.pdf)). Orphaned StructParents are the fingerprint of tags that existed in the source and were discarded during the merge. That rules out the alternative explanation that clerks simply uploaded untagged files. Municode's own marketing describes one-click packets and says nothing about tags ([Municode](https://www.municode.com/node/2081)).

**PrimeGov/OneMeeting drops attachment trees in both tenants tested, and in one it leaves a misleading tag tree.** The Oviedo packet has no tree at all, and the pages taken from two tagged attachments keep orphaned StructParents ([Oviedo packet](https://cityofoviedo.primegov.com/Public/CompiledDocument?meetingTemplateId=11327&compileOutputType=1)). The Florida researcher flagged a caveat: the paired Oviedo source lacked `/MarkInfo`. The Boulder evidence removes most of that doubt. Boulder's 199-page January 2026 packet declares **Marked=true**, but its structure tree covers only the three agenda pages. Forty-five attachment pages carry stale StructParents keys, and some of those keys collide with the agenda's own ParentTree entries. A screen reader that trusts the tree could therefore read agenda content while the user is on an ordinance page ([Boulder packet](https://bouldercolorado.primegov.com/Public/CompiledDocument?meetingTemplateId=1106&compileOutputType=1)). With two tenants and two independent researchers, this is the best-supported packet finding in the set.

**CivicClerk keeps source tag trees, but the packet still fails PDF/UA.** The two researchers' findings overlap, and the Florida paired tests are the stronger evidence. At Minneola, a tagged attachment kept its full subtree at packet pages 141–144. The ParentTree entries matched the source element-for-element, and custom roles were merged into the RoleMap ([Minneola packet](https://minneolafl.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=9492,plainText=false))). At Alachua, a Word-made staff report kept its 15 H2 headings and element-level EN-US. This answers the CivicPlus researcher's open question about whether headings survive: they do, at least in these two packets. Both researchers independently found the same defects across the four CivicClerk packets inspected (Alachua, Minneola, Wildwood FL, Dubuque IA). The packet catalog **drops `/Lang`**, and the Florida researcher also saw `DisplayDocTitle` dropped and the title left empty. Every page also receives a **"Page X of N" stamp drawn outside any marked-content sequence**, so it is neither tagged nor an artifact ([Alachua packet](https://alachuafl.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=2341,plainText=false)); [Wildwood packet](https://wildwoodfl.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=5868,plainText=false))). The Alachua packet adds a worse defect, seen in 1 of 2 paired packets: an off-by-one ParentTree mapping on packet pages 11–14, so MCIDs on two pages resolve to the wrong structure elements. Untagged and scanned sources stay untagged, with no OCR. A JPEG exhibit was wrapped as a Figure whose alt text was its filename. The pages CivicClerk generates itself use layout tables and contain no headings. An Ada file therefore keeps its internal structure in a CivicClerk packet, but the packet as a whole would fail PDF/UA whatever the clerk uploaded.

**Diligent re-tags rather than preserves.** Oldsmar's 199-page package has 19,276 structure elements: 19,230 /P, 45 /Link and one /Document. It has **no headings, tables, lists, figures or `/Alt`** anywhere, and its "ITEM n." and page-number stamps are tagged as content spans, not artifacts. Pages that were untagged in the per-item PDF are tagged in the package, which points to tags being generated at build time. The XMP still declares **pdfuaid part 1** ([Oldsmar package](https://myoldsmar.civicweb.net/document/254805/CITY%20COUNCIL%20-%2015%20Sep%202026.pdf?handle=F0A883CDDACB423389FA57A4FA2EEFA1)). Diligent's own wording matches this: its tools "do their best at maintaining the accessibility of the source documents" but "cannot be relied upon to create fully accessible PDF documents" ([Diligent fact sheet](https://connect.diligent.com/file-asset/Diligent_Community_ADA_Accessibility_Fa?v=1)). It is an inference, not an observation, that an Ada file's headings and alt text would be flattened. No richly tagged source could be isolated in that package.

The remaining platforms produced no inspectable compiled packet that contained attachments. CivicPlus Agenda Center's builder packets were untagged at Battle Creek MI and Wethersfield CT, but neither contained attachments ([Battle Creek packet](https://battlecreekmi.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_04272026-3174?packet=true)). Lake Placid FL builds its packet itself in Acrobat. The result has a 1,223-element tree but `/Marked false`, and it misuses "Artifact" as a structure type 240 times. That loss comes from the clerk's workflow, not the platform ([Lake Placid](https://www.lakeplacidfl.net/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Agenda/_09212026-73)). Tampa's OnBase portal published no packets for any meeting from August to October 2026 ([Tampa Agenda Online](https://tampagov.hylandcloud.com/251agendaonline/)). Hyland's documentation describes one-PDF packets with configurable "text overlays" and does not mention tags ([Hyland: Configuring Packets](https://docs.hyland.com/r/OnBase/Agenda/English/Foundation-22.1/Agenda/Configuration/Configuring-Packets)). According to a help-article snippet, Legistar's documented packet workflow combines files in Adobe Acrobat ([Creating an Agenda Packet in Legistar](https://support.granicus.com/articles/How_To/Creating-an-Agenda-Packet-in-Legistar)). Peak documentation says packets "are always a collated PDF document" and that Office files are converted and appended, but it says nothing about tags ([Downloading an Agenda in Peak](https://support.granicus.com/articles/How_To/Downloading-an-Agenda-in-Peak)). A 2017 BoardDocs FAQ said the public printed packet came "without attachments" ([BoardDocs FAQ](https://www.njsba.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/06/BoardDocsFAQ.pdf)).

The vendors' own remedy is HTML, not tag preservation. CivicPlus sells DocAccess, which builds an HTML view of packets and states that "Original PDFs remain unchanged as the official record" ([CivicPlus blog](https://www.civicplus.com/blog/am/meet-accessibility-and-language-access-mandates-without-changing-official-records/)). Diligent licensed the same tool for Community, BoardDocs and iCompass on 2026-09-22 ([Diligent/DocAccess release](https://www.financialcontent.com/article/bizwire-2026-9-22-diligent-announces-integration-with-civicplus-docaccess-helping-public-sector-organizations-expand-access-to-supporting-documents-at-scale)). Granicus lists a tool to "evaluate, remediate, and validate PDFs" only as **Preview** ([Granicus innovation update](https://granicus.com/innovation/)). Granicus's Peak help, seen as a snippet, tells clerks that attachments "are not ADA compliant by default" and must be fixed before attaching ([Peak Agenda Accessibility](https://support.granicus.com/s/article/Peak-Agenda-Accessibility?language=en_US)).

## Word uploads hand tagging to the vendor's converter

When the clerk uploads a .docx, the platform's server-side converter decides how the document is tagged, and the converters observed were mostly configured badly. **PrimeGov** converted Word uploads with Aspose.Words 25.11 into untagged PDFs, both at Oviedo ([Oviedo G2](https://cityofoviedo.primegov.com/api/compilemeetingattachmenthistory/historyattachment/?historyId=58d61929-6dd5-44a7-a871-3756b95e4897)) and at Boulder ([Boulder memo](https://bouldercolorado.primegov.com/api/compilemeetingattachmenthistory/historyattachment/?historyId=02f4c826-b10f-4950-907f-7904e7040130)). **OnBase** at Tampa rendered a .DOCX attachment with Aspose.Words 23.11, also untagged ([Tampa O2](https://tampagov.hylandcloud.com/251agendaonline/Documents/DownloadFileBytes/CRA2512421.DOCX.pdf?documentType=1&meetingId=2934&itemId=26578&publishId=214185&isSection=False&isAttachment=True)). **CivicClerk** produces tagged output, but thinly. Minneola's converted minutes contained 139 /P elements and nothing else, yet declared PDF/UA-1 ([Minneola meeting API](https://minneolafl.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/1149)). Wildwood's converted resolution reached P/L/LI ([Wildwood packet](https://wildwoodfl.api.civicclerk.com/v1/Meetings/GetMeetingFileStream(fileId=5868,plainText=false))). The one inspected **Peak** staff report was an untagged UniDoc PDF, though it is unclear whether it came from a Word upload ([Burbank meta_id=481478](https://burbank.granicus.com/MetaViewer.php?view_id=6&event_id=9895&meta_id=481478)). **Legistar** is the exception. At Delray Beach the stored-file path served the raw .docx, while Fort Lauderdale's InSite served a Word-2016-made, tagged PDF (119 elements, en-US) through PLOP ([Fort Lauderdale memo](https://fortlauderdale.legistar.com/View.ashx?M=F&ID=15972374&GUID=5D95F1E6-6AB4-4639-9FDB-53DB1C005054&G=F11871F6-88F5-40B5-BD94-B6711694F9A7)). Diligent says outright that its Word-to-PDF processing cannot be relied on for accessibility ([Diligent fact sheet](https://connect.diligent.com/file-asset/Diligent_Community_ADA_Accessibility_Fa?v=1)).

Two of these converters stamped a PDF/UA-1 identifier on output that has no headings or tables: Diligent's packages and CivicClerk's Word conversions. That matters for how Ada Editor makes its case. A PDF/UA claim in a file's metadata, and even a passing automated check, does not show that the file has meaningful structure. Ada's pitch is stronger when it names what Ada preserves (headings, tables, alt text, reading order) than when it rests on the label.

## What Ada Editor should say, build and test next

**Guidance to clerks.** Ada should give clerks three rules, one for each finding above. First, upload the exported PDF, never the .docx: on PrimeGov, OnBase and CivicClerk the converter, not Ada, would decide the tagging, and it decided badly. Second, attach each Ada document as its own item attachment rather than relying on a clerk-assembled packet. Point residents to that link, and say in the agenda that accessible versions of each item are available there. Third, on platforms where the attachment link is absent or unreliable, post the Ada file on the town's own website and link it from the agenda item. That covers Municode Meetings, which shows only a packet, OneMeeting tenants that strip uploads as Boulder does, and Diligent tenants configured like Oldsmar. This matches what vendors already put on the customer. Granicus "cannot guarantee that these PDFs, or any attachments, will be ADA compliant" ([Granicus Acrobat guide](https://support.granicus.com/s/article/Identify-and-Fix-Accessibility-Issues-with-Adobe-Acrobat-Pro?language=en_US)), and Diligent makes uploaded content "the responsibility of the customer" ([Diligent fact sheet](https://connect.diligent.com/file-asset/Diligent_Community_ADA_Accessibility_Fa?v=1)).

**The sales pitch.** The pitch should be "accessible at the source, and it stays accessible wherever your platform passes the file through." It should not be "stays accessible on any platform." The packet is the vendor's artifact, and on Municode Meetings, OneMeeting and Diligent it will not carry Ada's structure. That limit is a selling point, not a weakness. CivicPlus and Diligent are answering Title II with paid HTML alternatives that leave the PDF untouched. Ada offers the official PDF record itself, structured correctly. Deadlines should be cited cautiously. Granicus's July 2026 release gives **April 26, 2027** for jurisdictions of 50,000 or more and **April 26, 2028** for smaller ones, which covers most small Florida towns ([Granicus press release](https://granicus.com/press-release/why-the-2027-accessibility-deadline-is-really-a-2026-problem-according-to-granicus/)). Vendor blogs still cite the original 2026/2027 dates ([CivicPlus](https://www.civicplus.com/blog/wa/verify-pdf-documents-are-fully-accessible/)), and nobody checked the Federal Register.

**Build first: a post-publication checker and one export hardening.** The highest-value feature is cheap. Given the public URL of a posted attachment or packet, Ada would fetch it and report whether the tag tree, language, title and PDF/UA identifier survived. It would compare the file with the Ada original, detect the orphaned StructParents fingerprint, and run veraPDF. This turns the tenant-dependent OneMeeting result and the untested Legistar pdfuaid question into an answer for each town, and it gives clerks evidence for their records. The export hardening comes from the Alachua finding: **element-level `Lang` survived the CivicClerk merge while the catalog `/Lang` did not**. If Ada writes `/Lang` on its top-level structure element as well as in the catalog, its files would likely keep their language inside CivicClerk packets. That is an inference worth confirming in the first platform test.

**Defer full packet assembly, and treat PDF upload as the real decision.** An Ada-built accessible packet would help only where the clerk controls the packet file. That is confirmed for Agenda Center upload mode, where Lake Placid's own Acrobat merge broke its tags, and for town websites. It is plausible for Legistar, whose documented workflow combines files in Acrobat. On Municode Meetings, CivicClerk and OneMeeting the platform generates the packet, and nobody verified whether a clerk can replace it. More importantly, real packets are mostly not Ada documents. They include scanned applications (Wildwood's 300-dpi page images), Distiller-made spreadsheets (Alachua's CIP table) and vendor invoices (Oldsmar). An accessible packet builder therefore requires Ada to import and remediate third-party PDFs, a separate and much larger product that would compete with CommonLook-style remediation, which CivicPlus already bundles ([Allyant–CivicPlus](https://allyant.com/company-news/allyant-partners-with-civicplus-to-empower-local-governments-with-pdf-accessibility-remediation/)). A narrower first version would merge structure trees correctly, stamp page numbers as artifacts, keep `/Lang` and the title, and refuse to claim PDF/UA for any packet that contains non-Ada pages. That version is a reasonable pilot for Agenda Center and website-posting towns once the checker shows demand.

**Verify next.** The single most decisive test is the one the Florida researcher proposed. A cooperating clerk on each platform uploads one known Ada PDF/UA test file (with headings, a table, alt text, `pdfuaid`, DisplayDocTitle), and the posted attachment and packet are compared with the original and run through veraPDF and PAC. In priority order, test CivicClerk first (heading and alt-text survival, the ParentTree bug, and the element-level `/Lang` fix), then Municode Meetings (whether a clerk can supply their own packet), then a second OneMeeting Florida tenant to settle the Oviedo/Boulder split, while asking Granicus what its 2026 "accessibility features" fix covers. After those come Legistar's PLOP pass (whether `pdfuaid` and DisplayDocTitle survive) and Diligent (whether a known rich source is flattened). eSCRIBE and BoardDocs need a person in a normal browser, since both blocked automated requests.

## Conclusion

The accessibility boundary in municipal publishing is not the file but the merge. Platforms mostly leave a clerk's PDF alone. They break it when they combine it with others, and the breakage depends on how each vendor configured a commodity .NET library: two vendors on the same Aspose library got opposite results. These are configuration choices, so they can change, as Granicus's release note about attachments losing "accessibility features" suggests. That gives Ada Editor an unusual position. With a checker and a standard test file, Ada can be the party that documents, for each platform, whether accessibility survives, and that evidence is useful to clerks, to vendors and in Ada's sales conversations.

The vendors' turn toward HTML alternatives (DocAccess on CivicPlus and Diligent, Granicus's Preview tool) reframes the competition. Ada is not competing to make packets accessible, a problem no vendor has solved in PDF. It is competing to be the accessible original that those HTML layers and packet links point back to. A packet builder can come later, and only as part of a deliberate move into PDF remediation. For now, the defensible claim is narrower and stronger: an Ada file uploaded as a PDF stays accessible on the attachment link, and Ada can show the clerk whether it did.
