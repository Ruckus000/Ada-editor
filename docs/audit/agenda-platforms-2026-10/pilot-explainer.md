# Do agenda systems keep accessible PDFs accessible?

**A four-to-six-week pilot for Florida city and town clerks**

The DOJ's 2024 rule under Title II of the ADA requires local governments to make the documents they post online accessible, agendas and minutes included. A well-made PDF carries hidden structure, called tags, that lets a screen reader announce headings, read tables and describe pictures. What nobody has published is whether that structure survives the trip through an agenda system.

## What we've found so far

We looked at public agenda files from nine Florida towns and cities on seven agenda systems:
- **A document posted as its own attachment** usually kept its structure.
- **The combined agenda packet** usually lost it, or lost the document's language.
- **Word files uploaded and converted by the system** usually lost it too.

This was a small sample, so we want to test it properly, on real systems, with real documents.

## What we're asking

1. Write **one item you'd post anyway** (your next agenda, a public notice or a staff memo) in Ada Editor, our accessible document editor.
2. Export it as a PDF and **post it the usual way**.
3. **Send us the links** to the item's own attachment and to the packet.

That's about fifteen minutes on top of your normal work.

## What you get

- **Ada Editor at no cost** for the pilot.
- **Credit in the published summary** if you'd like it. Otherwise your town isn't named.

## What this isn't

- **Not an assessment of your town.** We look only at the document you write in Ada Editor, never at anything else you've posted.
- **Not a sales call.** There is nothing to buy in this pilot.
- **Not a judgment of compliance.** No tool can determine legal compliance, and we never use the word. Our results describe what the software did to one file.
