# The Ada Editor logo, October 2026

**The question:** the app's icon was a placeholder, a white "A" on a blue tile. What should the mark be, and what is the product called?

**What I looked at:**
- The approved design direction from the [UI audit](ui-audit-2026-10.md): calm and editorial, one typeface (Atkinson Hyperlegible Next), the existing token palette, hairlines and straight edges.
- The README's stance that the app never tells anyone a document is "compliant".
- Four logo directions drawn side by side on a design canvas, each at 64, 32 and 16 px, in light and dark, and next to the name.
- The source files are in [`design-system/brand/`](../../design-system/brand/):
  - [`mark.svg`](../../design-system/brand/mark.svg) is the 64-unit master.
  - [`mark-16.svg`](../../design-system/brand/mark-16.svg) is the pixel-snapped 16 px master, with its dark-mode colours built in.
  - [`mark-glyph.svg`](../../design-system/brand/mark-glyph.svg) is the a and caret on their own, for the in-app tile.

## The one-line verdict

**The mark is a lowercase a with a text cursor beside it, on a blue tile, and the product is called "Ada Editor".** It says "a place to write" first and leaves the checking to the product itself.

## How we got here

1. **A brief.** I wrote down what the mark has to say (a writing tool that reviews your work, honest about its limits), where it has to work (a 16 px browser tab up to a social card), and what it must avoid.
2. **Four directions.**
   - **Caret:** a lowercase a and a text cursor.
   - **Margin note:** a page with a mark in the margin beside one line.
   - **Braille a:** the six-dot braille cell with dot 1 raised.
   - **Refined A:** the existing A, redrawn in the house typeface.
3. **The owner picked Caret** and settled the name as "Ada Editor".

## Decisions

### Caret, not a checkmark or a seal

The product's position is that a checker can't certify a document. A seal, shield or large checkmark would claim exactly that, so all four directions left them out. Of the four, Caret was the only one that reads as "editor" on sight and stays legible at 16 px.

**What would change it:** a move to a product that does certify or audit documents, where a verdict mark would be honest.

### Not Margin note

It told the "reviewer, not judge" story best, but its margin dot disappears at favicon size, and the mark relies on a second colour to work.

**What would change it:** a need for a mark that says "review" more than "write", for example if the product shifts towards checking other people's documents.

### Not Braille a

It was the most distinctive, but braille used as decoration can read as tokenistic to blind readers, and at 16 px the six dots blur into a texture.

**What would change it:** blind users, asked directly, saying they like it.

### Not Refined A

It's the safest option, but it's a generic letter in a tile and says nothing about the product.

### The name is "Ada Editor"

Two words, title case, no hyphen, wherever a person reads it: page titles, docs, script output and the PDF Creator field. Identifiers keep `ada-editor` (the package, the `.ada-editor` CSS class, the repository, the Supabase project), because renaming them would break things without changing anything a reader sees.

## The mark's rules

- **Construction:**
  - The a is outlined from Atkinson Hyperlegible Next Bold, so the mark never depends on a font loading.
  - The tile is 64 × 64 units with a corner radius of 8.
  - The caret is 5 units wide and 38 tall, 4 units from the a.
  - The a and caret are centred as one group.
- **Two masters:**
  - The 64-unit drawing is for 24 px and up.
  - The 16 px drawing is for 16–23 px. Its caret is snapped to a 2 px column and its a is set slightly larger.
- **Colours:**
  - Primary: `#1A4FBF` tile with a `#FFFFFF` a and caret.
  - Dark theme: `#A8C7FA` tile with `#12203A`.
  - One colour: `#161C24` tile with `#FFFFFF`.
  - No tile: `#1A4FBF` on a light ground.
  - Every pairing is above 3:1 against its ground; the primary tile is 7.2:1 on white.
- **Lockup:**
  - The name is set in Atkinson Hyperlegible Next Bold.
  - Text size is 0.6 × the mark and the gap is 0.3 × the mark, with the name centred on the mark.
  - The smallest lockup uses a 24 px mark.
- **Clear space:** a quarter of the mark's width on every side.
- **Home screen:** files are square and full-bleed with no radius, because the platform applies its own mask. The a and caret sit inside the 80% maskable safe circle.
- **Don't:**
  - Stretch the mark.
  - Colour the caret differently from the a.
  - Set the name in another typeface.
  - Hyphenate or lowercase the name.

## What shipped

- **Generated files:** `npm run icons:build` ([`scripts/build-icons.mjs`](../../scripts/build-icons.mjs)) creates every icon file from the SVG sources:
  - `app/icon.svg`, the 16 px master, which switches colours in dark mode.
  - `app/favicon.ico`, for browsers that can't use an SVG favicon.
  - `app/apple-icon.png`.
  - The manifest icons in `public/icons/`.
  - The social card, saved as both `app/opengraph-image.png` and `app/twitter-image.png`. Its text is set as outlines from the shipped fonts.
- **Manifest:** `app/manifest.ts` names the app and points home screens at the icons. Its `display` stays `browser`.
- **In-app tile:** the brand tile in the headers keeps its theme-aware near-black colour, which is the one-colour version, and now holds the Caret glyph. The blue version is for icons and the social card.
- **Emails:** both auth emails (`supabase/templates/`) show the Caret mark, built from table cells rather than an image so it still appears when an email app blocks images. It uses the blue tile in light mode and the dark-theme colours in dark mode. The templates are pasted into the Supabase dashboard by hand, so a change here reaches inboxes only after it's pasted there.

**What would change it:** if the app headers ever move to colour, swap the tile's background to the blue token and keep the glyph.
